# Supermarket-Sales-Statistical-Analysis


Supermarket Sales — Statistical & Behavioral Analysis Summary

🧭 Project Objective

The goal of this analysis was to understand shopping behavior patterns using statistical exploration, focusing on:

- distribution structure

- customer segmentation

- temporal dynamics

- interaction patterns

- payment behavior



Distribution & Statistical Foundations

Key Findings

Sales distribution is right-skewed.

Most baskets are small to moderate, while fewer large transactions drive the mean upward.

Mean > median across many segments confirms a long-tail structure.

Interpretation

Skewness is not random noise — it reflects:

- recurring high-value time windows
- planned shopping behavior
- environment-driven opportunities for larger baskets



Customer Type Behavior (Member vs Normal)

Observations

- Members show higher median and mean basket values.

- Basket variability is slightly higher for Members.

- Weekend spending increases for both groups, but more strongly for Members.



Time Behavior — The Core Structural Driver

Time emerged as the most foundational layer.

Weekday Structure

- Category activity is more fragmented across hours.

- Different product lines peak at different times.

- Suggests routine-driven, schedule-based shopping.

Weekend Structure

- A strong midday block (≈11–14) emerges.

- Multiple categories show elevated medians simultaneously.

- Indicates a shared high-value shopping environment.

Key Insight
Weekdays → segmented behavior
Weekends → synchronized behavior

Two Parallel Shopping Rhythms - one of the strongest discoveries of the project:

🔵 Traffic Rhythm (Frequency-driven)

High invoice counts

Moderate basket sizes

Broad temporal distribution

Stable across environments

Represents routine, habitual shopping flow.

🟠 Value Rhythm (Basket-driven)

Concentrated in midday windows

Higher medians and variability

Stronger on weekends

Drives the right tail of the distribution

Represents planned or extended shopping.



Payment Behavior — Behavioral Layer


🟢 Ewallet — System Stabilizer

Appears consistently across hours.

High invoice counts across weekdays and weekends.

Moderate medians.

Interpretation:

👉 Acts as a habitual baseline payment supporting traffic flow.

🔵 Credit Card — Adaptive & Situational

More temporally concentrated.

Expands into high-value midday windows on weekends.

Indicates context-sensitive usage.

🟠 Cash — Mixed Pattern

Broad presence but less structural identity.
Likely reflects availability rather than rhythm.

Integrated Behavioral Model


Combining all findings:

Time → primary structural driver
   ↓
creates two parallel rhythms:
   • traffic flow (stable)
   • value windows (adaptive)

Customer type amplifies value behavior
Payment methods reflect intent levels
Categories respond to time environments
